# Lighting research — weekly pass, 2026-10-28

Scope: real-time lighting / GI work published or updated roughly 14–28 Oct 2026, weighed against current
genos-engine GI v2 bottlenecks. Sources checked: arXiv cs.GR recent listings, ACM TOG / DL, engine talk and
blog searches (UE 5.8 / MegaLights), JCGT and the SIGGRAPH Asia 2026 paper list (no lighting items indexed yet).
Two older items (May 2026) are included because they map directly onto open problems; they are marked as such.

Bottlenecks this pass is judged against:

- Moving-light frame cost: direct ~9 ms and atrous ~6 ms of ~22.6 ms moving lighting on an RTX 4070
  (moving-lighting breakdown, 20 Oct 2026); 8 ms moving-gate p99 cap fails on master.
- Lamp-turn pops and stale surface-light-cache texels after light changes (`../surface-cache-wall-leak.md`).
- Light leaks through thin walls.
- ~200 MB lighting VRAM soft budget; floor GPU GTX 1060 6 GB / RX 580; ray query optional.
- No baked lighting, lightmaps or trained fields standing in for the live answer (ADR 0012).
- Near-camera error vs the path-traced reference matters most; no flicker or pops.

## Prototype proposals

### (A) Reliability-weighted dual-rate blend for cache entries and screen probes

- Idea: keep a fast and a slow running estimate per cache entry (and per probe tile for screen probes), plus an
  exponentially weighted correlation between the cache prediction and each new live sample. Low reliability
  (a lamp just turned or moved) → use the fast estimate; high reliability → the slow one.
- Targets: lamp-turn pops, stale surface-light-cache texels after light changes, flicker in still scenes. It
  complements localized invalidation (`../localized-invalidation-design.md`) instead of relying on a scene-wide
  hash change.
- Cost: ~2–4 floats per cache entry (well inside the VRAM budget), no extra rays; runs on the floor GPU.
- Measure: moving gate (s1–s6 p99), a pop metric on lamp on/off (max per-pixel luminance step vs the reference
  over N frames), still-scene flicker, and error vs path-traced reference near the camera.

### (B) Split-Radiance-Cascades-style hit-distance interval split

- Idea: split cache and screen-probe rays into near and far intervals by hit distance; store near intervals at
  fine cache cell size near the camera and far intervals coarser, reusing the existing hash table and pages.
  Per-interval visibility gives a cheap occlusion signal for thin walls.
- Targets: near-camera error vs the reference, thin-wall leaks (lit_views base 61.4 vs cap 43.8), and less
  reliance on heavy atrous filtering.
- Cost: extra slots for near intervals — budget-check against the ~200 MB soft cap before going further;
  ray count unchanged.
- Measure: VRAM per preset, moving ms per pass, `lit_views` leak metric, and near-camera error vs reference.

Recommended order: (A) first (cheap, directly hits pops and stale texels), then (B).
