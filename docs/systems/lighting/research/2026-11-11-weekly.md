# Lighting research: weekly pass, 2026-11-11

Scope: real-time lighting and GI work published or updated from about 4 to 11 Nov 2026, compared against the current
lighting-v3 / integration state (PR #252 and the stage PRs stacked on it). Sources checked: arXiv cs.GR recent listings
(4 to 10 Nov), the SIGGRAPH Asia 2026 paper list and project pages (official publication date 9 Nov), ACM DL, JCGT,
Graphics Programming Weekly, and engine release notes (UE 5.9 Preview, 9 Nov).

Bottlenecks this pass is judged against (from the 10 to 11 Nov 4070 and lavapipe runs in the roadmap):

- The world-cache fill hits a ray ceiling under change. On yaw and moving lamps, fill stays on the 1.40M-ray ceiling for
  roughly the first 100 frames because whole sets are re-traced. The High cap-8 trial (#262) cuts still-hall lighting
  to 41% of 64-set cost on lavapipe. On the 4070 it gives 48 vs 42.5 fps on yaw, but the yaw and moving-lamp peaks do
  not change.
- Speed: Anthony measured about 70 fps walking on lighting-v3 vs about 55 fps on the integration branch (4070, 1440p).
  The targets are about 500 fps for the basic scene and 100 fps for the stress scene.
- Grain on flat and far surfaces, and direct-term speckle on a static scene. #263 adds a running mean for the direct
  term. It is under review.
- Catch-up after lamp changes: #259 and #261 drive the display catch from the A-SVGF lambda. Off->on reaches 90% in
  9 frames.
- ReSTIR DI cost still misses its perf gates. Speed work is allowed in follow-ups.
- Constraints: about 200 MB of lighting VRAM, a GTX 1060 / RX 580 floor with ray query optional, and no baked or
  trained fields standing in for the live answer (ADR 0012).

## Prototype proposals

### (A) Screen-tile fixed-budget world-cache fill (Lumen World Cache style)

- Idea: replace per-cell set scheduling during change with a fixed per-frame path budget seeded from the screen: one
  pixel per N×N tile, chosen with blue noise and rotated each frame. Trace up to K bounces. Store emissive + one
  sampled light + the hit's cache read at each vertex, and terminate the last bounce on the previous frame's cache.
  Cell averaging stays capped (8 to 16, per the cap sweep). Unvisited cells age out. The per-cell due list stays only
  as a low-rate background top-up for cells off screen paths.
- Targets: the 1.40M-ray ceiling on yaw and moving lamps, fps under motion, and near-camera fill speed after cuts.
  Cost becomes proportional to screen tiles, not to changed cells.
- Risks: far and flat cells reached rarely by paths may get noisier, which could make the grain worse. Test 8×8
  against 4×4 tiles. Cells only reached by indirect paths converge more slowly.
- Measure on the 4070 under the bench lock: yaw and moving-lamp ms and rays (p50 and p99) against #262 cap 8,
  still-hall error against the path-traced reference near the camera, the far-grain heat map from #255, and
  lamp-change frames to 90% (#258 harness).

### (B) Energy-based lamp culling before ReSTIR DI candidates and cache hit lighting

- Idea: before candidate generation, drop lamps whose bounded contribution at the shading point, from
  intensity × falloff × cosine bound, is below a fraction of the strongest lamp's bound (or below an absolute
  luminance floor). Use the same test at world-cache hits.
- Targets: ReSTIR DI initial-pass cost on the 32-lamp oracle scene and the hit-lighting share of the direct and
  hit_light timing.
- Risk: bias if the bound is not conservative. Gate on oracle lamps-spin and lamps-switch grid against the reference,
  plus the s7 noise gate.

Recommended order: (A) first, because it targets the biggest measured gap (motion ray peaks and walking fps), then (B)
as a small follow-up on the ReSTIR speed track.
