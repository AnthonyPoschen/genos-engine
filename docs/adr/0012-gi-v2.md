# GI v2: meshes, two tracers, a surface cache and screen probes

Status: accepted as the direction on 2026-10-10. It supersedes [ADR 0008](0008-lighting-uses-radiance-cascades.md) and [ADR 0011](0011-occluders-use-a-sparse-camera-box.md) when phase 6 of the migration lands. Until then those two still describe the shipped code. The full design is [GI v2](../systems/gi-v2-design.md).

Most future assets are arbitrary meshes, not our analytic shapes. Lumen is the proven reference we learn from, not a blueprint: we copy its per-mesh SDFs, hardware ray queries, mesh cards and screen probes, and diverge where its choices cause pops, lag or ghosting. Each mesh gets a signed distance field and a set of cards at import. A software tracer walks mesh SDFs near a ray's start and a world-anchored far SDF beyond. A hardware tracer uses Vulkan ray queries on a BVH when the GPU has them. Both sit behind one interface, and everything above it is shared. Light is cached on mesh cards (the surface cache) and in world probes, and the final picture is gathered by screen probes on visible surfaces. Direct light is traced per pixel through the same tracer, with a light tree for many lights and cached shadow masks for static lights. Emissive surfaces light the scene through the cache, and bright ones are area lights.

We keep our own rules where Lumen is weak. What is stored never depends on the camera; the camera only orders the work. Each moving light has its own layer, so on, off and dimming are free. Moving objects carry their own cards and an object-space proxy, and static GI ignores them. One priority score picks the work, each update finishes, then blends once. Screen probes reuse visibility across frames, never averaged light, so there is no ghosting.

Lighting may use 100-200 MB of VRAM (about 10-20 MB today), and spends it to make each pixel cheaper: cached static light, cached shadow masks, persistent world probes with pre-integrated irradiance, precomputed probe visibility and distance fields. No radiance history is kept.

The hardware floor follows the Steam survey: the newest 80 % of users. In September 2026 that is the 2018-2019 class (Turing, RDNA 1, Intel Xe, Ryzen Vega APUs). The floor is Vulkan 1.3 with descriptor indexing and buffer device address, and 4 GB of VRAM on discrete GPUs. Hardware RT is optional. The reference path tracer, compare and the regression scripts remain the quality gate, run on meshes and on both tracers.

**Rejected:** Keeping analytic shapes as the trace target. Hardware RT as a requirement (about a quarter of users and macOS have none). Software distance fields only (RT is 72 % of users and is faster and sharper). Copying Lumen wholesale. Lumen's temporal accumulation of radiance. Camera-centred clipmaps and camera-distance card resolution, which change stored light as the camera moves. Shrinking caches on the floor preset; the floor card is short on compute, not memory. Ray tracing pipelines instead of ray queries. A third-party glTF, image or BVH crate.
